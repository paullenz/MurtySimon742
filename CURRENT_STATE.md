## Preserved full mathematical argument: profile-defect capacity envelope

This appendix is a complete mathematical transcription of the original proof in the evidence bundle, not a claim of byte identity with its PROOF.md. All conclusions remain internal and conditional on actual canonical selected representatives.

### A. Input, residual labels and actual profile defects

At a B-source u, write N_u=S_u disjoint-union R_u. Let F be the A-side auxiliary graph. The inherited union implication for a selected obligation (u,i)->v is

    N_F(i) subset R_u union R_v.                         (A1)

Different selected labels at one source use different destinations. For each candidate receiving vertex v, let gamma_v be a proved nonnegative integer upper bound on incoming monitored obligations. The canonical rho_v+b-a-1, or a valid larger bound, suffices. If a comparison changes profiles, either recompute these ceilings or use bounds valid for the whole comparison; do not assume capacities stay unchanged.

Fix a core C, monitored ACTUAL selected incidences E, their source set U, and a certified superset V of all their destinations. The main receiver inequality permits U and V to overlap. It does not permit assuming that every monitored arc is internal when some may go elsewhere.

Let J be the relevant off-core labels, namely the union of N_F(i) over monitored incidences, minus C. Define

    n_(u,j)=#{i:(u,i) in E and j in N_F(i)},
    D_U=sum_(u in U)|R_u minus C|,
    B_V=sum_(v in V)|R_v minus C|.

These are source-label and receiver-label incidences, not counts of selected omissions, eta, beta or damaged bundles. If j is residual at u, one source incidence discharges all n_(u,j) requirements for purposes of (A1). Otherwise all those monitored obligations require j residual at their destinations, and those destinations are distinct for that source.

### B. Minimum receiver support for one residual label

Given a nonnegative source-load vector a and nonnegative receiver capacities gamma, sort both decreasingly. Define L_gamma(a) as the smallest t from zero through |V| satisfying

    sum_(r=1)^k a_r <= sum_(v=1)^t min(gamma_v,k)
    for every k=1,...,|U|.                              (A2)

The zero vector has value zero; if no t works, use infinity.

Necessity follows by taking the k largest source loads. One receiver can receive at most one relevant obligation per source, and at most gamma_v in total. Thus its contribution is at most min(gamma_v,k). Choosing the t largest capacities gives an upper bound on what any t receivers could provide.

For sufficiency in this SINGLE-LABEL COMPLETE-COMPATIBILITY projection, join a super-source to each source u with capacity a_u, every source to every one of the t receivers with capacity one, and each receiver to the sink with capacity gamma_v. For a cut whose retained source subset is X, the best receiver-side contribution is sum_v min(gamma_v,|X|). The cut has total capacity at least sum a precisely when sum_(u in X)a_u is no larger than this contribution. For fixed |X|=k, the largest left side is the prefix sum in (A2). Thus the cuts suffice, and integral max-flow supplies a binary source-receiver matrix.

This standard cut fact is not a new flow theorem. The projection deliberately ignores full destination compatibility, labels on other obligations, and even a possible source-equals-receiver prohibition if U and V overlap. Relaxing these restrictions can only lower the required number of receiver incidences, the safe direction here.

When all receivers have uniform positive integer ceiling Gamma, the exact projected value is

    L_Gamma(a)=max(max a,ceil(sum a/Gamma)),               (A3)

provided that value does not exceed |V|; otherwise it is infinity. For k<=Gamma, each of the largest k loads is at most the displayed value. For k>=Gamma, the prefix is no greater than the total, itself at most Gamma times that value. This proves all (A2) cuts. Zero capacities are handled directly by (A2).

Nonuniform example: source loads (2,2) and receiver capacities (3,1) have equal total four and two candidate receivers, but fail at k=2 because min(3,2)+min(1,2)=3. The first receiver cannot take two obligations from either one source, so total capacity alone is insufficient.

### C. Optimal relaxed placement of source-profile defects

For each j, sort its positive source loads as a_(j,1)>=...>=a_(j,p_j)>0 and define

    f_j(k)=L_gamma(a_(j,k+1),...,a_(j,p_j)),
    k=0,...,p_j.                                       (A4)

Deleting k source-label cells can remove at most k of these loads. The r-th largest surviving load is at least a_(j,k+r). L_gamma is monotone in its loads, so the most favourable k deletions remove the k largest loads. This is an exchange argument. We conservatively permit every positive-load cell to become residual even if another known selection would actually prohibit that; the extra freedom weakens the conclusion and is not an attainability claim.

Let P=sum_j p_j. For a nonnegative integer defect allowance D, define

    Psi(D)=min sum_j f_j(k_j),
      0<=k_j<=p_j integers, sum_j k_j<=D.                (A5)

For D>P, Psi(D)=Psi(P)=0. Infeasible terms are infinity. A finite multiple-choice dynamic program computes (A5): process labels, keep the least cost for each exact total of deleted cells, then take prefix minima. A greedy marginal-gain rule is not justified: loads (1,1) with ceiling two give f=(1,1,0), so the second deletion helps more than the first.

**Theorem.** D_U<=D implies

    B_V>=Psi(D).                                       (A6)

For the proof, let k_j count positive-load sources actually carrying residual j. The corresponding source-label incidences are distinct, so sum_j k_j<=D_U<=D. At least f_j(k_j) receivers must carry j. Different j occupy different receiver-label incidences, even at a shared receiver. Sum those incidences once to get B_V>=sum_j f_j(k_j)>=Psi(D).

Multiple obligations may share the same receiver-label incidence. They are not charged once per arrival: (A2) controls that reuse using the actual per-source injectivity and receiver capacity. Nor is a receiver's whole residual degree charged once per label.

However the projected minima for different j may choose inconsistent destinations for the same obligation. Hence Psi is an exact optimum only for the displayed independent-label relaxation, NOT for the full joint routing or any original graph. This retained limitation is important. A projected feasible optimizer is not a canonical witness.

### D. Safe conversion to one source-and-receiver budget

At exact source defect D_U, retain B_V>=Psi(D_U). If U and V are DISJOINT,

    sum_(w in U union V)|R_w minus C| >= D_U+Psi(D_U).

If only D_U<=D is known, a safe bound is

    sum_(w in U union V)|R_w minus C|
      >= min_(0<=d<=min(D,P)) [d+Psi(d)].                (A7)

For D>P the same range suffices: d>P costs at least P, and d=P already gives P. Irrelevant source residual labels only increase D_U.

For overlapping U,V, their common-vertex outside-core contribution must be subtracted from D_U+B_V. Without control of that overlap, retain (A6) alone. Also

    sum_(v in V)(rho_v-1)
      =B_V+sum_(v in V)|R_v intersect C|-|V|.

Thus B_V is not automatically a residual-excess bound. A baseline and core terms must be retained before inserting the result in the global ledger. No new sigma_O constant, d=5 threshold or catalogue exclusion is asserted.

One source residual incidence permits a loss of two projected receiver incidences but leaves a bound of seven. At two source defects the lower bound is five. ### F. Explicit nonzero-profile-defect control and why its scope matters

For integer g>=3, use g monitored labels I, g-1 core labels C, and one external label j. Take F to be complete bipartite between I and C union {j}. There are 2g A-labels. Use one source u, g receivers v_i and g-1 support vertices w, so there are 2g B-vertices and b-a-1=-1.

Initially set

    R_u=C, S_u=I union {j};
    R_(v_i)=(I minus {i}) union {j}, S_(v_i)=empty;
    R_w=C union {j}, S_w=empty.

Send monitored (u,i) to v_i for i in I. Each monitored label has F-degree g, residual degree g-1, demand one and one selected occurrence. Each core label has residual degree g and demand zero. Label j has residual degree 2g-1, so its demand is zero although it is selected at u.

Change the source profile by moving j from S_u to R_u, keeping all monitored selections intact. Remove j from all receiver residual sets. Each monitored label still has demand one and the same destination. The core demands remain zero; j now has residual degree g and demand zero. Exactly one source residual incidence outside C has been added.

Before and after the change, all label inequalities x_i>=s_i hold. Every selected source incidence satisfies demand eligibility and A-side domination. Every monitored arc satisfies destination absence, forward companion presence, reverse containment, residual union and source-destination injectivity. Its receiver has incoming capacity at least one; after the change that capacity is g-2>=1.

The receiver j-cost drops from g to zero; total receiver outside-core cost drops from g^2 to g(g-1), a loss of g, while source outside-core cost rises by one. Thus these local conditions cannot imply a universal one-for-one cost loss per defect. The one-label load is (g), and (A4) correctly gives f_j=(g,0).

The omitted conditions are explicit: the initially selected unmonitored j has no assigned full route, and no original edge-critical graph is established. The formal surplus e(F)-r is 1-g^2 initially and g-g^2 after the change, both negative. This is NOT a Murty-Simon counterexample and does not refute a positive-surplus/full-routing strengthening. The g=3..8 controls test exactly the conditions just stated.

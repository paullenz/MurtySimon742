# Residual-one k=2 W_s-free private-spoke theorem

Date: 2026-09-20

Status: **same-session raw-criticality strengthening**, conditional on `r=1`, `k=2`, `J2=empty` and the hostile-replayed residual-one hub/private-orientation package. No finite scan is used.

The D1 nonhub argument in fact does not depend on having exactly one K-neighbour. It extends to every W_s-free escape that has at least one K-neighbour, including the K-heavy reservoir.

## 1. Setup

Let `K={a,b}`, `W_s={z_a,z_b}`, and let t be an escape vertex with

`N(t) cap W_s=empty`, `N(t) cap K != empty`.

Fix a private coordinate `i in I` with `t q_i in E`.

## 2. Private-spoke criticality has the same two outcomes

Apply raw rooted B-edge criticality to `t q_i`.

### Forward orientation

The A-witness must contain q_i. At `J2=empty`, h_i is its unique X-neighbour and no Y-vertex contains q_i. Therefore

> **`N(t) cap N(h_i)={q_i}`.**                            `(WF-F)`

Since every Y-vertex is adjacent to h_i,

> **`N_Y(t)=empty`.**                                     `(WF-Y0)`

### Reverse orientation

The A-witness r must be adjacent to t and nonadjacent to q_i, with

`N(q_i) cap N(r)={t}`.                                   `(WF-R0)`

No K-neighbour of t can serve as r. If h in K is adjacent to t, then its selected witness z_h is adjacent to both h and q_i, so z_h is an extra common neighbour. A K-nonneighbour of t is unavailable by definition. A Y-witness is impossible because h_i is a common neighbour of q_i and every Y-vertex. The head h_i itself is adjacent to q_i.

Hence r is forced into `H\{h_i}`:

> **there is `h_l in H\{h_i}` with `N(q_i) cap N(h_l)={t}`.** `(WF-R)`

Thus every W_s-free escape private spoke is either Y-killing forward or H-consuming reverse.

## 3. Reverse spokes see every other q_i-neighbour

Let `T_i` be any collection of W_s-free escapes adjacent to q_i. Suppose `V_i subseteq T_i` are reverse-oriented at i. For each `t in V_i`, choose its forced witness h(t).

For fixed i the map `t -> h(t)` is injective because the pair `(q_i,h_l)` has one graph-fixed common-neighbour set. Also, for every `t' in T_i\{t}`, the edge `t'q_i` forces

`h(t)t' notin E`.

Therefore

> **`Z_{H,T_i} >= |V_i|(|T_i|-1)`**                      `(WF-RECT)`

counting the missing pairs generated by the reverse witnesses at coordinate i.

This applies simultaneously to D1 and K-heavy q_i-neighbours.

## 4. Consequence for D1/K-heavy overlap on private coordinates

Let N be a D1 q_j-nonneighbour set and R a K-heavy set. For coordinate i write

`n_i=|{t in N:tq_i in E}|`, `r_i=|{w in R:wq_i in E}|`.

If a D1 incidence at i is reverse with witness h_l, `(WF-R)` forces h_l to miss **every** K-heavy q_i-neighbour. Hence each such reverse incidence creates an additional located H--R block of size r_i, subject only to the same fixed-i witness injectivity already used in `(WF-RECT)`.

In particular, in the singleton-support endpoint where at most one D1 vertex uses i, a reverse D1 spoke at i costs at least

> **`r_i` H--R holes.**                                   `(WF-CROSS)`

Therefore a cheap near-matching of D1 vertices to private coordinates must avoid coordinates carrying substantial K-heavy q_i incidence, unless it chooses the forward orientation and pays the full Y-hole block instead.

## 5. Strategic consequence

The permissive sparse-support endpoint is now a **packing problem between two physical support systems**:

- D1 private supports;
- K-heavy private supports.

A coordinate used heavily by both sides is expensive in either private-spoke orientation. Thus any cheap asymptotic model must nearly separate the private-coordinate supports of D1 and K-heavy vertices, or make both support systems globally sparse.

The next natural test is the residual hub on the K-heavy side. Positive density of K-heavy q_j-neighbours should itself create an H--R rectangle, forcing the genuinely cheap K-heavy mass to miss q_j as well. After that, the escape reservoir is driven toward a sparse-support code-packing normal form.

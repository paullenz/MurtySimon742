## 1. Setup

Write

Let t be a D1 vertex with

`N_K(t)={a}`, `N(t) cap W_s=empty`, `t q_j notin E`.

> `S(t)={i in I : t q_i in E}`.                          `(NS-SUPP)`

Because t misses `q_j`, its U-code agrees with d at j; relative to d, its support is exactly `S(t) subseteq I`.

`N(z_h) cap N(h_i)={q_i}`

`N(q_i) cap N(h)={z_h}`

## 2. A D1 support can use only forward cells in its K-neighbour row

Suppose `i in S(t)`. If `R(a,i)` held, then

`N(q_i) cap N(a)={z_a}`.

But t is adjacent to both `q_i` and a, so t would be a second common neighbour. Contradiction.

Hence

> **`S(t) subseteq F_a:={i:F(a,i)}`.**                   `(NS-ROW)`

For a b-type D1 vertex the symmetric statement is `S(t) subseteq F_b`.

This is already a direct coupling between the escape U-code and the K x I private-orientation matrix.

## 3. Criticality of a private spoke `t q_i`

Fix `i in S(t)`. The edge `t q_i` lies in the rooted B-layer. Raw rooted edge criticality has the two standard orientations.

### Forward orientation

The A-witness must contain `q_i`. At `J2=empty`, the private endpoint `q_i` has the unique X-neighbour `h_i`, and no Y-vertex contains it. Thus the witness is forced to be `h_i` and

> **`N(t) cap N(h_i)={q_i}`.**                            `(NS-F)`

Every vertex of Y is adjacent to h_i. Therefore any Y-neighbour of t would be an extra common neighbour. Hence

> **a single forward private spoke forces `N_Y(t)=empty`.** `(NS-FY0)`

### Reverse orientation

Now the A-witness must be adjacent to t and nonadjacent to q_i, with singleton equation

`N(q_i) cap N(r)={t}`.                                   `(NS-R0)`

The witness cannot be a:

- a is adjacent to t, but `z_a` is adjacent to both a and q_i, so the singleton would fail;
- b is not adjacent to t;
- a Y-vertex is impossible because h_i is adjacent to both q_i and every Y-vertex;
- h_i itself is adjacent to q_i.

Since the X-layer is `K disjoint_union H`, the witness must be a matched head `h_l` with `l!=i`. Therefore

> **there exists `h_l in H\{h_i}` with**
> **`N(q_i) cap N(h_l)={t}`.**                            `(NS-R)`

Thus every private support incidence of a nonhub D1 vertex has exactly one of two costs:

1. forward: it kills the entire Y-neighbourhood of t;
2. reverse: it consumes a matched head in a graph-fixed singleton equation.

## 4. Reverse private spokes create a repeated-coordinate hole bill

Let N be a set of q_j-nonneighbour D1 vertices. Put

`L=sum_{t in N}|S(t)|`.

Let `N_F` be the vertices of N having at least one forward private spoke and write `f0=|N_F|`. By `(NS-FY0)`,

> `Z_{Y,N}>=f0(p-1)`.                                    `(NS-YBILL)`

Discard all private incidences belonging to N_F. The remaining incidences are all reverse. Their number satisfies

`L_R >= [L-f0(p-1)]_+`.                                  `(NS-LR)`

For each coordinate i, let

- `tau_i=|{t in N : i in S(t)}|`;
- `v_i` be the number of retained reverse incidences at i.

For fixed i, distinct reverse vertices require distinct witness heads `h_l`: one fixed graph pair `(q_i,h_l)` has one common-neighbourhood set and cannot have two different singleton heads. Moreover each such reverse witness h_l misses every other D1 vertex t' with `i in S(t')`, since t' is adjacent to q_i. Hence coordinate i creates at least

`v_i(tau_i-1)>=v_i(v_i-1)`

missing H--N incidence triples.

Summing over `p-1` private coordinates and using Cauchy,

`C:=sum_i v_i(tau_i-1)`
` >= sum_i v_i(v_i-1)`
` >= L_R^2/(p-1)-L_R`.                                  `(NS-TRIPLE)`

A single physical missing pair `(h_l,t')` can be generated by at most `|S(t')|<=p-1` coordinates. Therefore the number of **distinct** H--N holes satisfies

> **`Z_{H,N} >= max{0, [L_R^2/(p-1)-L_R]/(p-1)}`.**       `(NS-HBILL)`

No scan or probabilistic averaging is used: the only multiplicity loss is the explicit factor `p-1`.

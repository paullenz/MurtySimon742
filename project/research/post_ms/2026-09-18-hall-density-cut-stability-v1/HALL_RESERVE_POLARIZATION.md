# Hall reserve expansion and polarization

Date: 2026-09-18

Status: exact algebraic corollary of the Hall-density formulation in `HALL_DENSITY_STABILITY.md`.

For a complementary-pair family `X`, put

> `Sigma_X := W_X-a_X(a_X-T0)`,

where

> `W_P=Ccap_P+L_P-Z_P+R_P/p`,
>
> `T0=a-p`.

The capacity Hall theorem is exactly `Sigma_X>=0` for every family `X`.

## 1. Exact reserve expansion

Because `W` is additive while the demand is quadratic, for every family `X`,

> `Sigma_X`
> ` = sum_{P in X} Sigma_P`
> `   -2 sum_{unordered {P,Q} subseteq X} a_P a_Q`.       `(RE)`

Thus the complete Hall-cut system is equivalently

> `sum_{P in X} Sigma_P`
> ` >=2 sum_{P<Q in X}a_Pa_Q`                              `(RC)`

for every family `X`.

Interpretation: each singleton pair carries a local Hall reserve `Sigma_P`; when several pairs are grouped, those reserves must pay the quadratic interaction cost generated by A-edges between different pair populations.

This makes precise why singleton pair inequalities were insufficient: they verify only `Sigma_P>=0` and ignore the cross-pair product bill.

## 2. Disjoint-block polarization

For disjoint pair families `X,Y`, writing `x=a_X`, `y=a_Y`, `(RE)` gives

> `Sigma_{X union Y}=Sigma_X+Sigma_Y-2xy`.                 `(POL)`

Since the union must be Hall-feasible,

> `Sigma_X+Sigma_Y>=2xy`.                                 `(POL+)`

If `x,y>0`, at least one of

> `Sigma_X/x >= y`,
>
> `Sigma_Y/y >= x`                                        `(POL-D)`

must hold. Two disjoint macroscopic blocks therefore cannot both lie close to Hall equality.

For a partition `X_1,...,X_k` of a family of total A-mass `A`,

> `sum_i Sigma_{X_i}`
> ` >= A^2-sum_i a_{X_i}^2`.                              `(PART)`

Thus dispersing the A-layer among many separately low-reserve blocks is intrinsically expensive.

## 3. Relation to structural slack

The exact structural decomposition from the companion note is

> `Sigma_X=2E_X+M_X+J_X+kappa_X`,                          `(STR)`

where

- `E_X` is selected-source export across the A-cut;
- `M_X` is missing A-cut mass;
- `J_X` is unused direct/Hamming credit;
- `kappa_X` is unused non-direct certificate capacity.

Consequently `(POL+)` says that for any two disjoint large pair families, the sum of these four concrete defects must be at least twice the product of their A-populations.

This is a compact structural obstruction: a putative above-threshold graph cannot split its A-layer into two large regions that are simultaneously nearly complete across their external cuts, nearly source-closed, and nearly capacity/direct-credit saturated.

## 4. Whole-layer identity

For all pair classes, selected-source export and missing-cut mass vanish. Put

> `J=sum_P J_P`, `kappa=sum_P kappa_P`.

Then

> `Sigma_all=J+kappa`.                                    `(ALL0)`

Applying `(RE)` to all pairs yields

> `sum_P Sigma_P`
> ` = a^2-sum_P a_P^2+J+kappa`.                           `(ALL1)`

So the excess of the summed singleton reserves over the complete cross-pair interaction bill is exactly the global unused direct credit plus unused certificate capacity.

This identity is mainly diagnostic, but it identifies the two precise reservoirs that can prevent the Hall system from becoming tight.

## 5. Strategic use

The most promising use is together with threshold families from `HALL_DENSITY_STABILITY.md`. A near-maximal low-capacity family has tiny `Sigma_X`; `(POL+)` then forces its complement to carry almost the entire `2a_X(a-a_X)` reserve bill. At the structural level that means the complement must pay through selected-source export, missing A-cut mass, unused direct credit, or certificate-capacity slack. Each of those resources is already separately constrained elsewhere in the project.

No assertion is made that `(RC)` alone closes the eventual second-extremal problem. It is an exact repackaging designed to expose where a future beta/source or residual argument must bite.

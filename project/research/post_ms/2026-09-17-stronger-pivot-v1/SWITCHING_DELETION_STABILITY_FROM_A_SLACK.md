# Switching-deletion stability forced by the second-extremal slack budget

18 September 2026. Research directed by Paul Lenz; derivation by ChatGPT/Geeps.

**Status: internal candidate structural theorem; not externally reviewed.** This note abstracts the zero-signing-subcore theorem into a global switching invariant of the matched tight-pair core.

The conclusion is strong: unless the unmatched set is already quadratically large, any hypothetical graph above `M(n)` must have a matched 2-lift that remains nontrivial after deleting almost all but `O(sqrt(p+u))` coordinates.

---

## 1. The row sign graphs all lie in one switching class

Fix arbitrary labels on the tight fibres

`P_i={(i,0),(i,1)}`, `i=1,...,p`,

and encode the matched 2-lift by a sign graph `L` on `[p]`: an edge `ij` means the fibre matching is crossed in these labels.

For `y in U`, relabel each fibre so that its endpoint adjacent to `y` is called `q_i`. If `c(y)` is the Boolean choice vector relative to the fixed labels, then this relabelling is exactly a switching of the sign graph by the coordinate set represented by `c(y)`.

Thus

> `L_y = L Delta delta(c(y))`,                                  `(SD1)`

where `L_y=bar K_y` is the row sign graph and `delta(S)` is the complete cut generated by `S`.

In particular every unmatched row has the same switching class. Quantities defined by minimising over switching are therefore **global matched-core invariants**, not row-by-row accidents.

---

## 2. Switching-deletion number

Define

`kappa_sw(L) = min_{S subseteq [p]} tau_vc(L Delta delta(S))`,   `(SD2)`

where `tau_vc` is ordinary graph vertex-cover number.

Equivalently, `kappa_sw(L)` is the least number of tight-pair coordinates that must be deleted so that, after switching, every remaining fibre pair is parallel. Put

`sigma_0 = p-kappa_sw(L)`.                                      `(SD3)`

Then `sigma_0` is the maximum number of tight fibres supporting a zero-signing induced subcore in some switching state.

A second equivalent formulation is:

> `kappa_sw(L)` is the minimum `|D|` such that `L-D` is a cut graph on the remaining coordinates, i.e. a complete bipartite graph between some bipartition of `[p]\D` (allowing one side empty).

This follows because switching by one side kills precisely that cut.

---

## 3. A-side slack lower bound from switching-deletion distance

By definition there is a switching state with a zero-signing subcore on

`sigma_0=p-kappa_sw(L)`

fibres. If `sigma_0>=3`, the zero-signing-subcore quadratic slack theorem gives

> **SWITCHING-DELETION A-SLACK THEOREM.**
>
> `L_A >= sigma_0(sigma_0-1)`                                   `(SD4)`
>
> `      =(p-kappa_sw)(p-kappa_sw-1)`.

This bound is completely independent of which unmatched row is inspected. The local cheap-row results are special cases:

- complete-bipartite row sign graph: `kappa_sw=0`;
- star-plus-isolates: `kappa_sw<=1`;
- two-centre leaf graph: `kappa_sw<=2`.

---

## 4. Stability forced by an above-M candidate

Retain

`c_lambda=ceil(lambda(lambda+2)/2)`

and

`S_req=p lambda+3p+u lambda+2u-c_lambda-2`.

The exact parity gap says

`m>M(n) ==> E_U+L_A <= S_req-2`.                               `(SD5)`

Since `E_U>=0`, `(SD4)` forces the necessary condition

> `sigma_0(sigma_0-1) <= S_req-2`.                              `(SD6)`

Therefore

> **SWITCHING-DELETION STABILITY THEOREM.** Any near-full above-`M(n)` candidate satisfies
>
> `sigma_0 <= floor((1+sqrt(1+4(S_req-2)))/2)`,                 `(SD7)`
>
> and hence
>
> `kappa_sw >= p-floor((1+sqrt(1+4(S_req-2)))/2)`.              `(SD8)`

Whenever `S_req-2<0`, no above-threshold candidate exists at all.

The bound `(SD8)` applies for `sigma_0>=3`; if the displayed root is below 3 then the conclusion is stronger, namely that no zero-signing subcore of size three can occur.

---

## 5. Hardest near-balanced layer lambda=-1

At `lambda=-1`,

`S_req-2=2p+u-4`.

Thus an above-threshold candidate must satisfy

> `(p-kappa_sw)(p-kappa_sw-1) <= 2p+u-4`.                       `(SD9)`

Equivalently,

> `kappa_sw >= p-floor((1+sqrt(8p+4u-15))/2)`.                  `(SD10)`

If `u=O(p)`, this is

`kappa_sw = p-O(sqrt(p))`.

So in the genuinely near-full regime the matched signing cannot merely have a few local defects. It must remain switching-nontrivial after deleting all but only `O(sqrt(p))` coordinates.

This sharply separates the surviving regime from the entire fixed-defect/full-tight hierarchy previously closed.

---

## 6. lambda=0 and fixed lambda

At `lambda=0`,

`S_req-2=3p+2u-4`,

so

> `(p-kappa_sw)(p-kappa_sw-1) <= 3p+2u-4`.                      `(SD11)`

Again `u=O(p)` forces `kappa_sw=p-O(sqrt(p))`.

More generally, for every fixed `lambda>=-1` and `u=O(p)`, the exact formula for `S_req` is `O(p)`, hence

> `m>M(n) ==> kappa_sw=p-O(sqrt(p))`.                            `(SD12)`

This is an eventual structural stability statement on the **negative** side: a counterexample would have to be highly switching-complex, rather than a perturbation of the balanced/zero-signing core.

---

## 7. Why this is useful for the next attack

The row-kernel/Hall theorem says a cheap witness row has a bounded true-twin kernel. The new theorem says an above-threshold matched core in the near-full regime has switching-deletion number almost `p`.

These pressures point in opposite directions:

- low row cover tries to compress the sign graph into a bounded cloned kernel;
- the second-extremal slack budget forbids any large induced part from becoming a cut after switching.

The next compact target should exploit this incompatibility quantitatively: prove that a row graph with small `tau(Psi(K_y))` necessarily has `kappa_sw` bounded in terms of that row-cover number. The exact `tau<=2` classification already gives the first data point (`kappa_sw<=2`). If one can show, for example,

`kappa_sw <= F(tau(Psi(K_y)))`

with `F(t)=O(t)` or even polynomial in `t`, then `(SD8)` would force **large row-cover number** in every above-threshold near-full graph. That large row cover can then be attacked through selected/Hall capacity and A-code multiplicity.

The published order-12/size-32 hostile control is full-tight (`u=0`) and is untouched.

No all-order second-extremal theorem is claimed.

# Residual defect and the maximum-triangle-root interface

17 September 2026. Research directed by Paul Lenz; derivation and finite diagnostic by ChatGPT/Geeps.

**Status:** internal structural reformulation plus finite diagnostic; not promoted. The finite diagnostic is not a proof of the general root lemma.

## 1. The correct signed coordinate is `delta=r-e(F)`

Retain the canonical complement-root setup. Recall

`t=m-b(n-b)`

and the exact ledger

`e(F)=r+t`.

Define the **residual defect**

`delta := r-e(F) = -t = b(n-b)-m`.                  (D1)

Thus an upper bound on `m` is exactly a lower bound on `delta`.

Let

`M(n)=floor((n-1)^2/4)+1`.

The Dailly–Foucaud–Hansberg stronger bound

`m<=M(n)`

is equivalent to

> `delta >= b(n-b)-M(n)`.                            (D2)

This makes the change from the old Murty–Simon target explicit: the old programme often tried to rule out `delta<0`, whereas the stronger conjecture asks for a **positive linear defect** when `b` is near `n/2`.

### Parity forms

If `n=2s` and `b=s+d`, then

`b(n-b)=s^2-d^2`, `M=s^2-s+1`, so

> `delta >= s-1-d^2`.                                (D-even)

If `n=2s+1` and `b=s+d`, then

`b(n-b)=s(s+1)+d-d^2`, `M=s^2+1`, so

> `delta >= s-1-d(d-1)`.                             (D-odd)

The right-hand side is positive exactly in the difficult near-balanced degree window. Once it becomes nonpositive, the older positive-surplus machinery becomes relevant again.

## 2. The selected count is a rooted triangle count

The canonical root `v` has maximum degree `b` in `G`. Since

`A=N_H(v)`, `B=V(H)\N_H[v]`,

we have simply

`B=N_G(v)`.                                           (T1)

A missing pair of `H[B]` is therefore an edge of `G[B]`. Every such edge `uw` gives the triangle `vuw` in `G`, and every triangle containing `v` arises uniquely this way.

The selected-representative construction chooses exactly one selected cross-edge for every missing unordered pair of `H[B]`. Hence

> `Q := #selected = e(G[B]) = # {triangles of G containing v}`.   (T2)

This gives the Hall machinery a direct interpretation: it routes the criticality obligations generated by **triangles through a maximum-degree root**.

In particular:

- `Q=0` iff the chosen maximum-degree root belongs to no triangle;
- if `G` is triangle-free, `Q=0` for every possible root;
- the expanded-`C5` equality family is triangle-free, so the selected/Hall system is not expected to encode those already-known equality examples.

The stronger conjecture is already known in the triangle-free D2C class. Therefore the natural remaining use of this project machinery is the triangle-containing branch.

## 3. Two universal lower bounds on the defect

For labels, minimum H-degree gives

`x_i >= d_i-R_i`.

Summing and using

`sum_i x_i=Q`, `sum_i d_i=2e(F)`, `sum_i R_i=r=e(F)+delta`,

gives

`Q >= 2e(F)-r = e(F)-delta`,

hence

> `delta >= e(F)-Q`.                                  (L1)

For sources, the canonical bound is

`p_u <= rho_u+b-a-1`.

Since `a=n-1-b`, put

`gamma:=2b-n=b-a-1`.

Summing `p_u` and using `sum p_u=Q` gives

`Q <= r+b gamma = e(F)+delta+b gamma`,

hence

> `delta >= Q-e(F)-b gamma`.                          (L2)

Therefore

> `delta >= max(e(F)-Q, Q-e(F)-b gamma)`.             (L3)

At `b=n/2`, `gamma=0` and this reduces to

`delta >= |e(F)-Q| >=0`.

When `b<n/2`, (L3) contains the ordinary maximum-degree/average-degree lower bound in residual coordinates; this observation is useful for normalization but is not claimed as a new extremal result.

## 4. Degree balance controls residual activity without using `t>0`

The pointwise source inequality can be rewritten as

> `rho_u >= p_u+n-2b`.                                (B1)

This has three regimes.

### 4.1 Below half degree: `b<n/2`

Then `n-2b>=1`, so every B-source satisfies

`rho_u>=1`.                                           (B2)

Thus residual activity holds **without any sign assumption on `t`**. In this branch, every old theorem whose use of `t>0` was solely to invoke residual activity can be re-read with (B2) in its place. In particular the h-index lower bound and the charging inequality have no intrinsic need for positive surplus once (B2) is supplied separately.

This matters for odd expanded-`C5` examples: their maximum degree is `floor(n/2)<n/2`, so zero sources are impossible even though `t<0`.

### 4.2 Exact half degree: `b=n/2`

This can occur only for even `n`. Then (B1) is

`rho_u>=p_u`.                                         (B3)

If `rho_u=0`, necessarily `p_u=0`.

Let Z be the set of zero-residual sources. A missing H-pair `{u,w}` inside Z must be selected in one of its two orientations. If it is oriented `u->w`, it contributes one to `p_w`; if oriented `w->u`, it contributes one to `p_u`. Both are impossible by (B3). Therefore:

> **When `b=n/2`, Z is a clique in `H[B]`.**           (B4)

Equivalently, Z is an independent set in `G[B]`.

For a zero source `u`, every A-neighbour is selected, so

`q_u=|N_A(u)|`.

Since `p_u=0`, its missing degree in `H[B]` is exactly

`q_u=|N_A(u)|`.                                       (B5)

This couples the zero-source component union from `SIGNED_SURPLUS_PIVOT.md` directly to its B-side missing degree.

### 4.3 Above half degree: `b>n/2`

Put

`lambda=2b-n>0`.

A zero source obeys

`p_u<=lambda`.                                        (B6)

Again let Z be the zero-source set. Every missing H-pair internal to Z contributes one unit to `p` at exactly one endpoint. Hence

> `# missing pairs of H[Z] <= lambda |Z|`.             (B7)

Thus zero sources are still forced to be close to an H-clique when the maximum-degree excess `lambda` is small.

Also, for every zero source,

`missdeg_{H[B]}(u)=q_u+p_u=|N_A(u)|+p_u`,             (B8)

so its B-side missing degree differs from the size of its selected component union by at most `lambda`.

## 5. Relation to the signed zero-source lemma

The predecessor `SIGNED_SURPLUS_PIVOT.md` proves that a zero source `u`, with `S_u=N_A(u)`, satisfies

- `S_u` is a union of connected components of `F`;
- `e(F[S_u])<=delta` because `delta=-t`.

The present degree-balance lemma sharpens the interpretation:

- zero sources cannot occur at all below half maximum degree;
- at exact half degree they have no incoming selected B-pairs and are mutually H-adjacent;
- just above half degree their incoming selected-pair load is bounded by the small degree offset `2b-n`.

So multiple-zero-source component signatures remain a useful boundary problem, but they are **not** the universal central object of the stronger conjecture.

## 6. The triangle-bearing branch and the root issue

Dailly–Foucaud–Hansberg record that the stronger conjecture is already proved for triangle-free D2C graphs. Their conjectured large-order equality family `C5+` is triangle-free. Thus the unresolved structural work can focus on D2C graphs containing a triangle.

If such a graph has a maximum-degree vertex contained in a triangle, choose it as the canonical root. Then (T2) gives

`Q>0`,

so the selected-representative/Hall system is genuinely active.

This suggests the following high-value scope lemma.

> **Maximum-triangle-root question.** Does every triangle-containing D2C graph have at least one maximum-degree vertex that lies in a triangle?

If YES, the canonical framework loses no triangle-bearing graph by requiring `Q>0`.

If NO, the right repair is not to ignore the issue: choose a highest-degree triangle vertex and re-derive the complement-root machinery with an explicit degree slack measuring how far its H-degree is above the minimum.

## 8. External triangle structure worth importing carefully

Loh and Ma's work on diameter-critical graphs proves a useful fact about triangles: every triangle has at least two external critical-path “feet”, and each foot is adjacent to exactly one vertex of the triangle. This does not immediately prove the maximum-triangle-root statement, but it supplies controlled external vertices attached to every triangle and should be tested as the starting point for a degree comparison.

## 9. Next target

Take a triangle `T={x,y,z}`. Use the critical witnesses / feet forced by its three edges to compare the degrees of `x,y,z` with vertices outside T.

The desired lemma is:

`max{d(x),d(y),d(z)}=Delta(G)`

for at least one triangle T (or perhaps for every triangle; the small-order diagnostic is consistent with the stronger version but does not justify it).

If a degree comparison fails, preserve the exact obstruction. The fallback is a **near-minimum complement root** at a highest-degree triangle vertex with slack

`epsilon=Delta(G)-d(v)`,

and every place where minimum H-degree was used must then pay an explicit `epsilon`. That route would retain `Q>0` while making the scope loss auditable.

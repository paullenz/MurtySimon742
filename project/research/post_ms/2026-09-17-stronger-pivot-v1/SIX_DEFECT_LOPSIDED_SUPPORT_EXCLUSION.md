# Lopsided six-defect switching ray is support-impossible from k >= 9

18 September 2026. Research directed by Paul Lenz; derivation and finite core audit by ChatGPT/Geeps.

**Status: internal structural/certificate theorem; not promoted. External mathematical review and hand compression of the finite six-vertex core audit remain open.**

This is the first application of the coordinatewise residual-monotonicity lemma beyond five defects. It does **not** claim the complete six-defect layer. It closes the most weakly constrained unbounded ray: no isolated leaf-pairs and all ordinary leaves attached to one of the six exceptional coordinates.

The active target remains the eventual/sufficiently-large second-extremal D2C problem around

\[
M(n)=\left\lfloor\frac{(n-1)^2}{4}\right\rfloor+1.
\]

The order-12/32 `X_3` graph remains the separate `k=4,r=0` negative control.

## 1. Lopsided six-defect state

Let the switched graph have exactly six non-leaf coordinates `0,1,2,3,4,5`. Take coordinate `5` as the pendant root and attach

\[
z=k-6
\]

ordinary leaves to it, with no other pendant group and no isolated leaf-pair. Thus the attachment vector is

\[
(0,0,0,0,0,z).
\]

For `z>=2`, the exact leaf package consists of the two complementary cliques generated by same-group leaf pairs and has

\[
B_6=2(z-1). \tag{1}
\]

The residual ray-monotonicity lemma applies from `z=2` onward.

## 2. The base z=3 finite core audit

The first eventual base is

\[
z=3,\qquad k=9.
\]

There are `2^15=32768` labelled graphs on the six exceptional coordinates. Discarding cores for which an allegedly exceptional coordinate would actually have total degree one leaves

\[
15572
\]

valid labelled cores.

For every valid core, reconstruct the exact orientation-code graph from

\[
[c(q\to j)]=[N_L(q)\triangle\{j\}],
\]

delete the exact leaf-package code vertices, and solve the residual graph `R` exactly.

The additive lower bound

\[
B_6+\tau(R)-2k
\]

is strictly positive for every valid core except 15 labelled cores. Those 15 cores are all one rooted isomorphism class under permutations of `0,1,2,3,4` fixing the pendant root `5`.

In lexicographic exceptional-pair bit order

`01,02,03,04,05,12,13,14,15,23,24,25,34,35,45`,

a canonical representative has mask

\[
\boxed{27551}. \tag{2}
\]

Its exceptional-core edges are

\[
01,02,03,04,05,14,15,23,25,35,45. \tag{3}
\]

Equivalently:

- the pendant root `5` is universal in the six-vertex exceptional core;
- vertex `0` is also universal;
- the remaining four vertices induce `2K_2`, with edges `14` and `23`.

Thus the dangerous exceptional core is

\[
\boxed{K_2\vee 2K_2}, \tag{4}
\]

with one vertex of the joined `K_2` distinguished as the pendant root.

Every other rooted core is already support-impossible at `z=3`, and coordinatewise residual monotonicity propagates that strict excess to every `z>3`.

## 3. Exact all-z decomposition of the unique dangerous core

For the core (4), direct forced-code grouping gives the following component decomposition of the **full** orientation-code graph for every `z>=2`:

1. four two-vertex components whose physical edge has multiplicity two; total cover contribution `4`;
2. two cliques `K_{z+1}`; contribution `2z`;
3. eight stars `K_{1,z+2}`; contribution `8`;
4. one balanced double-star with two centres, `z+1` leaves at each centre, and a centre edge of physical multiplicity four; contribution `2`.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\tau(\Omega)=4+2z+8+2=2z+14.} \tag{5}
\]

Since `k=z+6`,

\[
\boxed{\tau(\Omega)=2k+2.} \tag{6}
\]

So even the unique core not killed by the disjoint leaf-package/residual lower bound lies two witness codes beyond the above-threshold support budget.

## 4. Lopsided six-defect theorem

Combining Sections 2 and 3:

> **LOPSIDED SIX-DEFECT SUPPORT EXCLUSION — internal candidate.** If a full tight-antipode switching class contains a six-defect state with no isolated leaf-pairs and all leaves attached to one exceptional coordinate, then for
>

This is a support theorem; no F-separation cleanup is required.

## 5. A real lower-order boundary obstruction

At `z=2`, so `k=8`, support alone cannot close every lopsided six-defect switching state. For example the **empty** six-vertex exceptional core is valid (the pendant root has total degree two and the other five exceptional coordinates have degree zero), and exact calculation gives

\[
\tau(\Omega)=16=2k. \tag{7}
\]

No D2C realization is asserted. The point is methodological: a blanket six-defect support theorem beginning at `k=8` would be false at the switching level. The threshold `k=9` above is not merely an artefact of the proof.

For the dangerous `K_2\vee2K_2` core itself, formula (6) already gives strict excess at `k=8`; the boundary equality comes from a different exceptional core.

## 7. Strategic consequence

Five defects were support-impossible throughout from `k>=8`. At six defects there is a finite boundary equality at `k=8`, but the lopsided unbounded ray is again support-impossible from the next order onward.

The next task is not to scan arbitrary six-defect attachment sizes. Coordinatewise residual monotonicity reduces the remaining six-defect geometry to finitely many `{0,1,2}` attachment-status patterns (with a small boundary adjustment to reach the chosen `k` threshold). The useful question is whether every such pattern has a finite residual matching/cover certificate, or whether another genuine low-support normal form survives and needs an F-separation treatment.

## Trust boundaries requiring mathematical scrutiny

The missing-neighbour argument must apply to every actual selected label, including labels of zero minimum demand. Source matching must retain mandatory labels while allowing additional incidences. Column choices remain associated with their demand labels; independently sorting residual columns would be unsafe.

Each iteration of pruning uses necessary supersets and previously valid bounds. A column option is removed only if it has no witness for at least one source row. A surviving option may have different witnesses for different rows. That last relaxation is deliberate and remains the principal structural gap, not evidence of a realized graph.

The activation inequality normalizes each incoming charge by a valid incoming-capacity upper bound. Summing the unnormalized cost would double-count activation at a shared supplement; a saved negative control demonstrates the failure. Lower bounds on outgoing selections are not assumed equalities. Choosing exactly the required number of label incidences is only a subset of actual selections, not an assertion that extra selections are absent.

The network is a necessary relaxation. It omits some source and undirected pair coupling and uses safe minimum degrees from column domains. This weakens rejection power rather than restricting actual graphs. Every accepted rejection is a cut or a verified integer potential inequality; numerical solver status is not proof evidence.

The infinite family excludes whole demand/residual rows and all compatible column refinements, not every graph at orders 44,48,52,... . The separate F and residual graphical realizations in the tests are not simultaneous constructions of the selected quasi-edge system.
